CLAIMS:
P01: Despite wide use, the effect of AI tools on real-world software development has not been well studied.
P02: The authors ran a randomized controlled trial to measure how early-2025 AI tools affect the productivity of experienced open-source developers.
P03: Sixteen developers with moderate AI experience completed 246 tasks in mature projects they had worked on for about five years on average.
P04: Each task was randomly assigned to either allow or forbid the use of early-2025 AI tools.
P05: When AI was allowed, developers mainly used the Cursor Pro code editor and Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet.
P06: Before starting, developers predicted that allowing AI would cut their completion time by 24%.
P07: After the study, developers still believed that allowing AI had cut their completion time by 20%.
P08: Allowing AI actually made developers take 19% longer to complete tasks, so AI tools slowed them down.
P09: The measured slowdown contradicts the predictions of economics experts (39% faster) and machine learning experts (38% faster).
P10: The authors examined evidence for 20 features of the study setting that could have contributed to the slowdown.
P11: The factors examined included project size and quality standards and developers' prior experience with AI tools.
P12: Experimental artifacts cannot be fully ruled out, but the slowdown held up across analyses and is unlikely to be mainly caused by the experimental design.
P13: The authors caution readers not to overgeneralize from the results.
P14: The observed slowdown does not mean that current AI tools do not often improve developer productivity.
P15: There is evidence that developers' high familiarity with their repositories, and the repositories' size and maturity, contributed to the slowdown.
P16: The results are consistent with small new projects or unfamiliar codebases seeing substantial speedup from AI.
P17: The authors expect that more reliable, lower-latency or better-prompted AI systems could speed up developers in this setting.
P18: Preliminary evidence suggests fully autonomous AI agents using Claude 3.7 Sonnet can often implement the core functionality of issues in several of the study's repositories.
P19: Those AI agents typically fall short of meeting all requirements, for example leaving out documentation, failing style rules and missing key tests.
P20: If AI progress continues, significant speedup may soon appear in this setting, though that is not guaranteed.
